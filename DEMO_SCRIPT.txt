WHYX Demo Script — 2 to 3 minutes

0:00–0:20
WHYX closes the gap between a KPI movement and a defensible business decision. It asks whether a change is meaningful, what explains it, and what action is justified.

0:20–0:55
Show Revenue decline — West India as Executive. Point out the KPI cards, the −14% West India movement and the expected −3% to +3% range. Explain that the movement is materially outside the expected range.

0:55–1:20
Show competing hypotheses. Inventory shortage leads at 78%, while competitor pricing, churn and seasonality remain lower. Point out that the scores are deterministic PoC evidence scores computed from the CSVs, not LLM probabilities, and that counter-evidence plus lineage are visible. Marketing conversion is used as a clearly labelled market-pressure proxy.

1:20–1:45
Switch Persona to Regional Manager while keeping the same scenario. Explain the key distinction: the Executive receives a strategic decision; the Regional Manager receives an operational view scoped to West India, including category-level inventory/stockout priorities and a 48-hour monitoring loop. Point out the RBAC entitlement in the sidebar.

1:45–2:05
Show the human feedback controls. Explain that a business user can accept/reject the leading driver and that this becomes a governance/calibration signal rather than changing quantitative truth.

2:05–2:25
Switch to Low confidence — conflicting evidence. Show 43%, 41%, 38%. Explain that no hypothesis clears the 65% threshold, so WHYX abstains and proposes the next-best investigation. Switch persona to show the different operational narrative.

2:25–2:40
Show New KPI — sparse history. Explain that with only 11 days of history, WHYX avoids making an unreliable anomaly claim.

2:40–2:55
Open KPI Semantic Contract, Confidence methodology, RBAC / Entitlement and LLM vs non-LLM processing. Point out runtime telemetry: 0 model calls / 0 LLM tokens / $0 cost in this deterministic PoC. Finish with: Numbers are deterministic; narratives are generative.
